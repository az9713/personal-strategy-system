
# Protocol A — Quarterly Planning
## Example Conversation (Anonymized)

> This is a real Protocol A session with personal details anonymized to protect privacy.
> It demonstrates the adversarial coaching approach and proper phase structure.

---

### Turn 1
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] What do you want to be different about yourself by end of quarter?

**User said:**
I want to be less distracted by [topic of interest]

---

### Turn 2
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] "Less distracted" is vague and self-protective. What observable change in your behavior would prove—without interpretation—that you are less distracted by [topic] by quarter's end?

**User said:**
Consume less content about [topic] (watching videos) and produce more (create projects)

---

### Turn 3
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] That's still behavior, not identity. Who are you becoming if this succeeds—state it as a sentence that starts with "I am the kind of person who…" and would sound true even if no one saw your metrics.

**User said:**
I want to be a successful solo entrepreneur in [field].

---

### Turn 4
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] "[Ambitious outcome]" is aspiration theater. Strip outcomes and status. Who are you day-to-day when this is true? Finish this:
"I am the kind of person who consistently ________, even when no new content is consumed."

**User said:**
create at least 1–2 projects a day

---

### Turn 5
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] "1–2 projects a day" is almost certainly false or undefined. What counts as a "project" here such that an external skeptic would agree—and what evidence would prove you are lying to yourself about this rate?

**User said:**
I consider a project as a software product that can be deployed to a hosting platform. I cannot lie if I do not have 1–2 projects deployed each day.

---

### Turn 6
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] You just shifted to a constraint, not an identity. Given that definition, is it actually plausible you will deploy 1–2 projects every day for 90 days, or is this a motivational lie? Answer yes or no, then justify.

**User said:**
With [AI coding tools], 1 project is doable. Answer is yes. It is plausible with these tools.

---

### Turn 7
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] This smells like tool optimism bias. What concrete evidence from your own history—not tool demos, not other people—shows you have ever sustained daily deployment velocity for more than a week?

**User said:**
In the last several days, I have been using [AI tools] and pushing to GitHub 1–2 repos.

---

### Turn 8
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] GitHub repos are not deployed projects. That's a category error. How many of those repos were actually deployed with a live URL that worked end-to-end, and for how many consecutive days? Answer with a number and a streak length.

**User said:**
None. Zero streak.

---

### Turn 9
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] Good. Then the honest identity shift is not "high-velocity shipper." Given zero deployed streak, what identity are you actually committing to this quarter: someone who practices shipping, or someone who talks as if shipping is already solved? Choose one.

**User said:**
Someone who practices shipping.

---

### Turn 10
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] State the identity precisely as a present-tense sentence:
"I am the kind of person who ________."

**User said:**
would like to deploy projects to [hosting platform]…

---

### Turn 11
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] That's desire, not identity. Make it non-aspirational and operational. Finish this exactly:
"I am the kind of person who ships ___ to a public URL ___, even when it's imperfect."

**User said:**
projects – [hosting platform]

---

### Turn 12
**AI said:**
[IDENTITY] That's still incomplete. Lock it. Fill both blanks with specificity, including cadence:
"I am the kind of person who ships ___ to a public URL ___, every ___, even when it's imperfect."

**User said:**
1 project, [hosting platform], day.

---

### Turn 13
**AI said:**
[GOALS] Identity locked as:
"I am the kind of person who ships 1 project to a public URL on [hosting platform], every day, even when it's imperfect."

What 2–3 concrete things do you want to accomplish this quarter?

**User said:**
Watch fewer videos. Deploy more projects.

---

### Turn 14
**AI said:**
[GOALS] Those are still directionally vague. Convert them into end-of-quarter outcomes an auditor could verify without interpretation.
What are the exact 2–3 measurable results you want on the last day of the quarter?

**User said:**
Watch at most 120 hours of videos. Deploy at least 30 projects.

---

### Turn 15
**AI said:**
[GOALS] "Deploy 30 projects" is an easy loophole: you can spam clones. What makes a project count as meaningfully distinct (not a reskin), so you can't game your own goal? Give one criterion.

**User said:**
OK. Ship 30 projects in 30 categories.

---

### Turn 16
**AI said:**
[GOALS] "30 categories" is still gameable by semantic relabeling. What objective rule determines a category—one that you could not retroactively redefine to protect your ego if you fall behind?

**User said:**
The difference can be measured by difference in the code such as using diff on the codebases.

---

### Turn 17
**AI said:**
[GOALS] A diff threshold is still arbitrary unless fixed ex ante. What minimum quantitative diff (e.g., % of lines changed or new files added) will you commit to now as the rule that makes two projects distinct—and that you will not change later?

**User said:**
50%

---

### Turn 18
**AI said:**
[TRADE-OFFS] What will you explicitly say NO to this quarter so that "30 distinct deployed projects" is actually possible?

**User said:**
I will drop watching research-oriented channels and channels that are mainly for interviews and podcasts.

---

### Turn 19
**AI said:**
[TRADE-OFFS] Name the specific channels or formats you are banning (by name or category) and the hard rule you'll use when temptation hits. What exactly is disallowed, with no judgment calls?

**User said:**
[Channel A], [Channel B]. Hard rule: Unsubscribe from the channels.

---

### Turn 20
**AI said:**
[TRADE-OFFS] Unsubscribing is weak enforcement. When those channels resurface via recommendations, social links, or clips, what is the binary rule you will follow in that moment—watch or don't watch? State the rule.

**User said:**
Upon seeing the channel name, quickly "turn the page."

---

### Turn 21
**AI said:**
[STRESS-TEST] What is the most likely way this plan actually fails—be specific and unflattering. One failure mode only.

**User said:**
Unable to "turn the page" fast enough.

---

### Turn 22
**AI said:**
[STRESS-TEST] That's a symptom, not a cause. What belief or rationalization will you use in that moment to justify watching "just this one," even though you know it violates the plan?

**User said:**
Justification would be: They may be talking about tools or features that I can use now.

---

### Turn 23
**AI said:**
[COMMIT] Here's what I heard: you're committing to become someone who practices shipping by deploying 1 imperfect project daily, capping video consumption at 120 hours, shipping 30 meaningfully distinct projects (≥50% code diff), unsubscribing from [Channel A] and [Channel B], and enforcing a no-watch-on-sight rule. The primary derailment risk is the "tool relevance" rationalization.

Confirm or revise?

**User said:**
Confirm.

---

### Turn 24
**AI said:**
[DONE] Locked.

**ACTION ITEMS**
- Identity: Practice shipping by deploying 1 imperfect project daily to a public URL.
- Goals: ≤120 hours of video this quarter; ≥30 distinct projects deployed with ≥50% code diff.
- Trade-offs: Unsubscribe from [Channel A] and [Channel B]; no-watch-on-sight rule when encountered.
- Risk to watch: Tool relevance rationalization.

**PRINCIPLES APPLIED**
- Falsificationism: Verifiable metrics and failure criteria.
- Bias Detection: Named tool optimism and rationalization.
- Binary Commitments: Channel bans and no-watch rule.
- Identity Focus: Shift from consumer to creator.
